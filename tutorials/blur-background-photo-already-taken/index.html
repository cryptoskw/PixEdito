How to blur the background of a photo already taken

Separate the person from the scene, keep the portrait sharp and apply a controlled blur to the original background layer.

You can blur the background of a picture after it has already been taken. The most reliable editing method is to separate the person from the scene, place the transparent portrait above the original photo and blur only the lower layer.

PixEdito uses AI for one focused task: removing a portrait background. The blur itself is a normal editor effect. This distinction matters because you retain control over the strength, layers and final composition instead of relying on several unrelated automatic modes.

A soft background can make a portrait feel calmer and direct attention toward the face. It cannot restore a subject that was already out of focus, so choose the sharpest original and inspect the eyes before beginning.

Your photo stays local

The portrait cutout and layer effects are calculated in your browser. PixEdito does not upload the selected image to its own server for processing.

Step by step

Blur the background while the person stays sharp

  1. 01

    Open the portrait in PixEdito

    Choose the highest-resolution version of the photo. Keep it as the original bottom layer. A clear head-and-shoulders portrait with visible contrast between the person and scene normally produces the cleanest cutout.

  2. 02

    Remove the portrait background

    Open the Remove Background tool and choose Use current canvas. PixEdito loads the local portrait model, detects the person and displays the transparent result beside the original.

  3. 03

    Add the transparent person as a layer

    Select Add as new layer. The portrait cutout should appear above the original image. Do not delete the lower layer: it will become the blurred scene behind the sharp person.

  4. 04

    Blur the original background layer

    Select the bottom photo layer, then open the editor’s effects or filters and apply a blur. Increase the amount gradually. Moderate blur usually looks more natural than an extreme effect and keeps environmental context.

  5. 05

    Refine the edges and composition

    Zoom in around hair, shoulders, hands and clothing. If an edge looks rough, undo and try a cleaner original or make a small manual correction. Crop the image only after the layers are aligned.

  6. 06

    Export the finished photo

    Use PNG when you want lossless quality or transparency elsewhere. Use JPG for a smaller finished photograph with a solid scene. Keep the original file and, if possible, a layered working copy.

Better results

Make the blur look natural

  • Keep the face and eyes completely sharp.
  • Use less blur when the original scene is already far from the subject.
  • Check the result at both normal viewing size and 100% zoom.
  • Avoid heavy blur around transparent hair where the cutout edge becomes visible.
  • Reduce brightness or saturation in the background if it still competes with the person.
  • Match the crop to the final use before exporting.
Why this method works

Two layers solve two different jobs

The original layer preserves the full scene, while the upper transparent layer protects the person. Because the blur affects only the lower layer, the portrait remains sharp. You can also reposition the person, adjust the crop or add text between the two layers.

This workflow is more flexible than flattening the image immediately. If the background is too soft, undo the filter and try a lower value. If the person needs more space, enlarge the canvas or move the cutout without rebuilding the AI mask.

Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

How do you blur the background without blurring the person?

Remove the portrait background, add the transparent person as a new layer, then apply blur only to the original photo layer below it.

Is background blur an AI feature in PixEdito?

No. PixEdito’s only AI action is portrait background removal. Blur is a manual editor effect applied to the lower layer.

Can I blur any photo?

The blur effect can be applied to any image, but keeping one person sharp depends on a clean portrait cutout.

Will this fix a blurry face?

No. Background blur changes the scene behind the person; it cannot recover detail missing from an out-of-focus face.

Do I need an account?

No. The background remover and editor work without a PixEdito account.
