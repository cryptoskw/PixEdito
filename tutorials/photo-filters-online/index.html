How to apply photo filters online without over-editing

Improve mood and colour with filters, brightness, contrast, saturation and warmth controls.

A filter can change the mood of a photo quickly, but the best result often comes from a light filter followed by small manual adjustments. The goal is usually to improve the image without making skin, skies or products look artificial.

Work from the largest problem first. Correct an image that is too dark before increasing saturation, and compare the edited version with the original as you work.

Your image stays local

The selected file is processed inside your browser by the editor. It is not uploaded to an PixEdito server.

Step by step

How to do it

  1. 01

    Open a well-exposed original

    Use the highest-quality original available. A heavily compressed screenshot gives the editor less colour and detail to work with.

  2. 02

    Correct brightness and contrast

    Adjust brightness until important details are visible, then add a small amount of contrast to restore depth.

  3. 03

    Fine-tune colour

    Increase saturation carefully if colours look flat. Use warmth to make a cool image feel more inviting or reduce it when whites look too yellow.

  4. 04

    Preview filters at a lighter strength

    Choose a filter that supports the subject, then avoid stacking multiple strong effects. Natural skin and brand colours are useful reference points.

  5. 05

    Compare and export

    Compare with the original, take a short visual break if needed, then save the edited version separately.

Better results

Useful tips

  • Make several small changes instead of one extreme change.
  • Check whites and skin tones for unnatural colour casts.
  • View the photo at normal size before exporting.
Quick answers

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between brightness and contrast?

Brightness shifts the overall lightness. Contrast changes the difference between lighter and darker areas.

Why does my photo look too orange?

The warmth or saturation may be too high. Reduce warmth first, then compare the result with neutral objects in the image.
